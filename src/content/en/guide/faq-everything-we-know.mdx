This page answers the most-asked questions about Fields of Mistria (NPC Studio's cozy farm life sim RPG, out of Early Access on August 5, 2026 at $13.99 on Steam) using official pages, the community wiki, and documented reviews. Short version: it's a single-player game with a mod scene, a shipped 1.0 feature set including marriage and children, and a promised post-1.0 content roadmap.

## Is Fields of Mistria multiplayer or co-op?

No — Fields of Mistria is a single-player game. NPC Studio's official FAQ and store pages never list any co-op or multiplayer mode, and all reviewer coverage describes a solo farm life. (Confusingly for searchers: a "Coop" *does* exist in-game — it's the building where you keep chickens, ducks, and their eggs, with incubators inside.) The only way to "share" the game today is the unofficial mod described below.

## Is there a multiplayer mod?

Yes, but experimental: Nexus Mods host a fan-made **"Multiplayer" mod by D3Ulo** (first uploaded August 3, 2026; v4.1 by August 12, 2026) that syncs a host and client sessions. Its own changelog admits rough edges — earlier versions could overwrite marriage/dating status. It's free, marked "Safe to use" by Nexus scanning, and entirely unofficial: expect bugs, and back up saves before trying it.

## Can you mod Fields of Mistria?

Yes, there's an active Nexus Mods section for the game. The community-standard tooling includes the **"Mods of Mistria" installer** and general mod managers like Vortex, and YouTube tutorials cover installation. Popular categories: gameplay/utility mods, the cheats menu below, quality-of-life tweaks, and the fan multiplayer mod. Mods apply to the PC (Steam) version only — there are no consoles to mod yet.

## Are there cheat codes?

There are no official cheat codes documented on the Steam page, official site, or wiki. What exists is the community **"Cheats Menu" mod by caterpixie** on Nexus (v1.2.3, 495 endorsements, ~25k unique downloads): an in-game menu with debugger functions plus QoL cheats like infinite health/stamina/mana, unlimited talking and gifting, and a screenshot mode that pulls the camera back for a full-farm view. Again: PC only, unofficial, back up your saves.

## What did the 1.0 update add?

The big one: everything the late Early Access phase was still missing. Officially listed 1.0 content:

- NPC heart cap raised to 10, with Proposal & Wedding events and **marriage + children**
- Renown (town rank) cap raised to 100; the **Town Repair Storyline is now completable**
- Additional dungeon quests and rewards in the Mines
- Additional museum sets and rewards, more Request Board quests
- Stillwell and Zorel join the Saturday Market

The Mines Update (early 2026) beforehand finished the Mines storyline, added the final biome, a secret bachelorette, apiaries and terrariums, item processors, and raised the skill cap to 60.

## How does animal breeding work?

Breeding creates rare **color variants** of animals and is cosmetic-only — colors don't change production or output tiers. Feed both parents a heart-shaped treat from Hayden's Shop; a stork icon confirms success and the baby arrives (or hatches, for chickens/ducks) after 4 in-game days. Offspring color is rolled randomly within tier logic: same-tier parents (T(x)+T(x)) guarantee the next tier up; mixing adjacent tiers gives roughly a 50% shot at +2 tiers. The top **Tier 6 "seasonal" variants** are rare rolls from T5×T5 or better pairings. Reviewers estimate roughly 5–15 color variants per species, and rare colors feed the livestock side of the Animal Festival (the competition system was still "coming" during Early Access — its exact 1.0 state is (to be confirmed)).

## How does the Museum work?

The Museum in The Narrows has **4 wings — Archaeology, Flora, Insects, and Fish — organized into 82 sets with 409 total donations** (wiki counts at time of writing). Most sets need 5 specific items; only the Legendary Fish Set (4 items) breaks the rule. Rewards are milestone-based: what you get depends on how many sets you've finished in a wing, not which set. Donating has social payoff too — villagers react when you've contributed notable finds.

## How does fishing work?

Fishing is Animal Crossing-style, not a Stardew bar: the fish nibbles the bobber a few times before the real bite, and the hook timing window is forgiving — multiple reviewers call it relaxing. Fish live in oceans, rivers, and even the Mines (each biome has its own species, like the Deep Earth's Rockbiter), gated by season and weather, and virtually anything you catch counts toward the Museum's Fish Wing — the wiki tracks donation eligibility per species. Related tips from the pros guide: you can swim from day one (no stamina cost, but splashing near fish scares them), and diving spots with bubble rings hold treasure.

## Is Fields of Mistria like Stardew Valley?

It's the comparison everyone makes, and the honest answer from reviewers: same genre DNA, different execution. Official framing calls it a "spiritual successor to the Farm-Sim RPGs of the late 90s & onward" (Harvest Moon's era, not just Stardew). Concrete differences documented across sources: fishing is nibble-timing instead of a tension bar; you can jump and swim; shop ownership moves with NPC schedules (buy from unattended stalls); magic exists as big one-shot spells tied to Mines progress; writing and persistent NPC storylines are far heavier. One 50-hour 1.0 reviewer summarized it as "not just another pixel art Stardew copycat — it has its own sauce," while both reviewers agree Stardew players will feel at home fast.

## What platforms is it on, and how much?

PC only, on Steam: Windows and Linux, **$13.99 USD**, **Steam Deck Verified** (one of the Deck's top-played games). Switch is not out — the official FAQ says additional platforms are "being evaluated," so Switch/PS5/Xbox dates are TBD (to be confirmed). No physical or collector edition is announced. System requirements are trivial (6 GB RAM, GTX 460-class GPU, 2 GB disk) — see the [system requirements guide](/guide/system-requirements).

## What languages does it support?

Eight: English plus beta localizations (about 75% complete at 1.0) in Simplified & Traditional Chinese, French, Japanese, Korean, Russian, and Spanish, with refinement promised post-launch.

## Good beginner tips?

From Kotaku's first-timer guide and community threads: upgrade your bag immediately (the 1000-gold Basic Pouch, +10 slots, pays for itself), drop excess items on the ground rather than skipping pickups (they persist for days, and a Lost & Found service consolidates them later), talk to every villager every time you see them, grab the free daily soup at the inn, check item tooltips for museum icons before shipping anything, prioritize town-rank quests over pure farming early (seeds are expensive; quests fund them), and craft chests the moment the recipe unlocks. And relax — the game is explicitly designed without punishment pressure.
